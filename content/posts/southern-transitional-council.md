Something big happened in the southern part of Yemen on the 8th of December, 2025. A separatist movement that seeks a further degree of independence (and possibly separation) of the southern part of Yemen from the rest of it, known as the Southern Transitional Council, made a major military move to seize more territories in Yemen by lunching the Hadhramaut military offensive. In this attack, they were able to take custody of oil-rich territories and asserted control over all eight southern provinces. This marked the biggest development in Yemen since the civil conflict erupted more than a decade ago.
This situation genuinely sparked fears that the south would soon declare itself an independent state, as it used to be before its unification with Yemen in 1990.
At the same time, Saudi Arabia withdrew all of its troops from Aden, which is the chief city in the south of Yemen, home to the internationally recognized government. This appeared to be a significant step backwards for Saudi support.
Intense fighting ensued between them, such that the airspace of Yemen was briefly closed, interfering with aerial movements.
Overall, this has further complicated and divided Yemen’s already-splintered civil conflict. Experts have been explaining how this can be un-stable and make negotiations even more difficult.
