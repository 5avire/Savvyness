As if its tradition of political tumult needed further validation, Peru’s cycle of election, impeachment, and scandal reached a climactic turn of events with President Dina Boluarte being voted out of office on October 10, 2025, by a shocking 124-0 tally. What else could have triggered this shocking moment of collective political angst if not the government’s inept handling of a raging crime wave? When President Boluarte failed to turn up for a mandated impeachment hearing at Congress, the result was unprecedented unanimity.

The change occurred with light speed. The following morning, Jose Jeri, president of Congress, had taken an oath as interim leader, promising to stabilize a country that has experienced six presidents in almost as many years. Nevertheless, the streets bore witness to another tale. Thousands of Peruvians, tired of what they deemed a "parliamentary dictatorship," poured into Lima and Cusco streets. Although Boluarte was unpopular, there were concerns that a new era of institutional volatility had emerged, with no head able to remain in power long enough to rule.
