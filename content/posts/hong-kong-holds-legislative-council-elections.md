The Legislative Council election took place on December 7, 2025. The second such election was conducted after Beijing hauled Hong Kong’s electoral landscape in 2021 and only allowed "patriots" loyal to China to take part. The hopefuls were all vetted for the election, and no pro-democracy candidates participated after many were barred, jailed, or chose to boycott.
Supporters claimed it ensured stability even in the aftermath of the 2019 protests. Others saw it as undemocratic since there was no competition and the results were biased to favor the Beijing camps.
Meanwhile, voter turnout was low, at only 31.9% – a slight increase from 30.2% in 2021 but still near-record low participation levels – due to public discontent over a deadly fire the day before, which claimed more than 150 lives.
All of the 90 seats were taken by pro-establishment lawmakers, reflecting ongoing tensions between security measures taken by Beijing and democratic freedoms.
